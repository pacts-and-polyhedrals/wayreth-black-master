# Wayreth: The Black Master

A private **levels 1–20 Dragonlance campaign** for D&D 5e (2014 rules), built for Foundry VTT v12–v14 and dnd5e through 5.3.3.

The campaign begins on **18 Darkember 350 AC**. The War of the Lance is already consuming eastern Ansalon, Caramon Majere has died in the catastrophe of Silvanesti, and Raistlin Majere has only recently taken the Black. More importantly, Wayreth itself has rejected Par-Salian's authority and recognised Raistlin as **Master**.

Version **1.34.0** is the whole campaign on maps. **87 playable scenes** across all six arcs plus the Vingard Road, with generated battle maps, walls and lighting: **351 journal pins** covering every session beat the module describes, **351 player handout cards** that send to chat carrying no GM material by construction, **11,922 wall segments**, pre-placed bestiary tokens and scene-to-scene navigation from Vogler to the Abyss. **81 illustrated creatures**, 33 managed journals, 90 robe spells, 30 magic items, and six Crow legacy items with their own player reference journals.

## Campaign structure

| Arc | Levels | Default play | Focus |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **I — The Road to Wayreth** | 1–5 | 8–16 sessions | Vogler, Ispin, three roads, Forest, Test, Night of the Eye |
| **II — The Black Master's Errand** | 5–8 | 14 sessions (12–16 flexible) | War-road journey, Veyrath, Palanthas, Astinus, Shoikan |
| **III — The Spire of Lore and Doom** | 8–12 | 16 sessions (14–18 flexible) | Palanthas sandbox, Shoikan, Doom-spire megadungeon, Fistandantilus, Portal |
| **IV — The Long Return** | 12–15 | 13 sessions (12–15 flexible) | Silvanesti, Zhaman, Istar, Memory/Time/True Name components |
| **V — The War Beneath the War** | 15–18 | 12 sessions (10–14 flexible) | Black Host, Returned, Wayreth assault, High Clerist, Kitiara/Soth, Caramon |
| **VI — Master of Past and Present** | 18–20 | 10 sessions (8–12 flexible) | Fistandantilus, Wayreth's confession, two-Tower siege, Abyss, Takhisis |

The core narrative is continuous: Raistlin begins by trying to **bring Caramon back**. Fistandantilus's research makes the impossible increasingly possible; the Portal, Long Return, Black Host and Returned all grow from that single objective. The campaign's central question is not whether forbidden magic is automatically evil, but whether love, survival and security justify taking away another person's capacity to choose.

## Where to start

Run **Session 0**, then read **The Road to Wayreth**, **Arc I**, and the chosen route journal. Before the party reaches Wayreth, read **Campaign Bible**, **The Black Master of Wayreth**, **The Forest and Tower of Wayreth**, and **The Test of High Sorcery**.

From Arc II onward, the dedicated arc journal is the table runbook. Each session page contains:

- level and expected runtime;
- session purpose;
- opening image;
- people in play;
- a structured scene sequence;
- knowledge the players can obtain;
- tracker guidance;
- an end beat;
- a hidden narrative-thread note explaining what the session must pay forward.

This keeps the campaign narrative consistent across fifty-plus later-arc sessions rather than leaving the GM to reconstruct the connective tissue from summaries.

## GM campaign tracker board

Version 1.17.0 adds a dedicated **Campaign Trackers** panel directly above the calendar for the GM. Players never receive the tracker panel.

The six world-state trackers are:

- **Raistlin Trust** — whether he considers the party tools, trusted agents or companions;
- **Raistlin Control** — whether domination becomes his preferred solution to uncertainty and loss;
- **Fistandantilus Influence** — how deeply the Dark One's philosophy enters Raistlin's thinking;
- **White Robe Bond** — Raistlin's ideological/personal trust in the White Robe PC;
- **Wayreth Accord** — whether the Tower learns to recognise the party as part of High Sorcery's survival;
- **War State** — strategic pressure of the War of the Lance.

Each card has a slider and **− / +** buttons. Shift-click changes a tracker by 2; Alt-click changes it by 3. The board can collapse into a compact score strip. The book button opens **Campaign Timeline and Trackers**. Tracker changes are world-authoritative and GM-guarded; the read API also returns no tracker values to non-GMs.

Tracker instructions are also embedded directly in every Arc II–VI session so the state is updated when decisions occur rather than reconstructed at the finale.

## Calendar and moon widget

The v1.16 multi-world calendar work is retained.

- **Calendar systems:** Krynn/Solamnic, Krynn/Ergothi, Krynn/Elven, Toril's Calendar of Harptos, and Oerth's Common Year.
- **Date + time:** world date and minute-of-day are synchronized to connected clients.
- **Scene darkness:** scenes marked for calendar sync follow time of day; Krynn can optionally receive moonlight from the visible moon phases.
- **Calculated moons:** alignments are computed from each calendar's moon cycles rather than hard-coded.
- **Krynn sanction:** Solinari/Lunitari/Nuitari high/low sanction applies only to Krynn calendars.
- **Nuitari:** hidden from players unless the GM reveals it.
- **Player access:** players can see the calendar and moon information but cannot alter world date, time, moon offsets or calendar settings.
- **Docked mode:** the calendar sits above Foundry's player list and tracks that list's width. **Docked calendars intentionally do not resize.**
- **Floating mode:** each user may switch their own calendar to floating, drag it and resize it from the bottom-right grip. Position and scale are client-only.
- **GM tracker board:** when present, it is a separate section immediately above the calendar in both docked and floating layouts.
- **Road to Wayreth preset:** sets the opening to **18 Darkember 350 AC** so the campaign can cross into **1 Newkolt 351 AC** for the Night of the Eye.

The public module API is available at `game.modules.get("wayreth-black-master").api` and as `WayrethCalendar`. World mutations are GM-guarded. Tracker mutations are GM-guarded and `getTrackers()` returns `null` to players.

## Legacy items

Six **legacy items**, one for each Crow, each a single Item that grows with its bearer rather than a chain of separate documents to swap between:

| Bearer | Item | Identity |
|---|---|---|
| Alice | **The Crown of Little Wonders** | Creative problem-solving |
| Solanthis | **Ispin's Promise** | Preparation and protection |
| Jinx | **The Coin That Lands Sideways** | Push-your-luck magic |
| Sylas | **The Shepherd's Lantern** | Knowledge at a price |
| Clipper | **The Wayfarer's Knot** | Navigation and rescue |
| Kattal | **Ispin's Unmoving Nail** | Physical protection |

Each has four states — **Dormant** (Uncommon, levels 3–5), **Awakened** (Rare, 6–9), **Exalted** (Very Rare, 10–14) and **Legendary** (15+) — and 66 level-gated activities between them.

What Foundry handles by itself, once an item is on a sheet:

- **Features appear at the level they are earned.** Every activity carries a minimum character level, so a feature is not on the sheet before its band.
- **Superseded features disappear.** Where a later state replaces an earlier one, the old version carries a maximum level and stops being offered.
- **The charge pool resizes** at levels 6, 10 and 15 — 3, 5, 7 then 9 charges.
- **Recovery changes with it.** Dormant and Awakened refill completely; Exalted regains 1d6 + 1 and Legendary 1d8 + 1, so a bigger pool refills more slowly. The Shepherd's Lantern does all of this at dusk instead of dawn.
- **The Legacy Save DC** (8 + proficiency bonus + primary ability modifier) is read from the character sheet.
- **Chat cards show one feature.** dnd5e builds a usage card from the item's whole description, which on a legacy item is all four states at once, so the module rebuilds the card around the feature actually used — its name as the title, its own text as the body, buttons and rolls untouched.

Two things are deliberately manual, and **The Crow Legacies** journal says so: Clipper's climbing speed ships as a switched-off effect, because a movement effect cannot gate itself on level; and Sylas' *Not Yet* has no automatic recovery at all, because it recharges after seven days rather than on a rest.

The items live in **Wayreth — Crow Legacy Items** rather than loose in the Item Directory, since each is bound to a named character.

### Player reference journals

Each item also has its own journal, written for the player who carries it: a quick-reference page with the charge table and a level-by-level list of what unlocks when, one page per state, and a page of play tips and the rulings most likely to come up.

They are **generated from the same definitions as the items**, by `scripts/build-legacy-journals.mjs`, so a reference sheet cannot describe a version of a feature that no longer exists. Every action cost, charge cost, rest limit, duration and save DC printed in them is read back off the built activity rather than written out a second time, and the validator fails the build if any feature on an item is missing from its journal, appears on the wrong state's page, or if a roll-data reference such as `@prof` leaks into player-facing prose.

The six install **visible to the whole party**, since a module cannot know which player is which Crow. A GM who wants each of them private can set per-user ownership on the individual journals after install.

## Bestiary art

All 81 creatures carry their own art. Each Actor has a **1024x1536 portrait** on its sheet and a **512x512 token** on the canvas, sized in grid squares from the creature's own size entry — so a Baaz draconian is 1x1 and an ancient red dragon is 4x4.

The portraits are full-body figures on transparent backgrounds. Tokens are derived from them by `tools/make-tokens.py`, which pads each portrait out to a square rather than cropping it: a square crop of a standing figure cuts it off at the waist, and a portrait used directly as a token is squashed into the grid cell.

```bash
python3 tools/make-tokens.py assets/bestiary
```

Art is matched to creatures **by name, not by filename order**, and `scripts/build-creatures.mjs` refuses to write if any creature has no art or any art file has no creature. The validator additionally checks that each portrait's filename still normalises to its creature's name, so a reordered or renamed art set cannot silently shuffle the portraits.

**The Wayreth Bestiary: Illustrated** presents the whole set as twelve plates, one per group, each entry with CR, AC, hit points, size and type. It is generated from `content/creatures.json` by `scripts/build-bestiary-gallery.mjs`, so it cannot list a creature the compendium does not have or miss one it does. The named-character plate is the campaign's cast list and is the one page worth keeping to yourself.

## Journal updates and GM edits

The v1.10.1 fingerprint system is preserved. Module pages store a content fingerprint so automatic updates can distinguish an untouched managed page from a GM-edited page.

- untouched pages can be refreshed by new releases;
- GM-edited pages are preserved on automatic version updates;
- a manual **Reinstall Wayreth Content** action is protected by confirmation and can intentionally rebuild module-managed material.

Actor copies of managed spells/items preserve user state such as preparation, quantity, equipped/attuned state and spent uses while module data refreshes around them.

## Install

Unzip into:

`Data/modules/wayreth-black-master/`

`module.json` must be at the root of that folder. Restart Foundry after replacing an older copy and enable the module in the world.

On the next GM world load, version **1.32.0** installs/updates the 32 managed journals, 90 spells, 30 magic items, 6 legacy items, 78 bestiary entries and managed world compendia. The import-version bump adds the six player reference journals, and refreshes untouched campaign source pages without overwriting GM-edited pages.

For an immediate rebuild, use **Reinstall Wayreth Content** in the Journal or Item Directory.

## Development

Campaign journal sources live in `content/*.json`. The calendar/tracker UI is `scripts/wayreth-calendar.mjs` with `styles/calendar.css`. Journal and Item installation is handled by `scripts/wayreth-journals.mjs`.

Validation:

```bash
npm run validate:all
```

This runs three checks. The spell validator covers the robe lists and school matrices. The content validator checks versioning, routes, Session 0, spell/item integration, calendar regressions, multi-world support, tracker permissions and placement, later-arc session structure, installer registration, and the legacy items — charge formulas evaluated at every level, tier gates, enums, and that every feature appears on the right page of its player journal. The card validator pushes all 66 legacy activities through the chat-card rebuild against dnd5e's real card markup; it needs a DOM and skips with instructions if one is not installed:

```bash
npm install --no-save linkedom
```

Generated content is rebuilt with `node scripts/build-legacy-items.mjs` and `node scripts/build-legacy-journals.mjs`. Both refuse to write if anything is inconsistent, so run them before validating.

## Release

Create a GitHub release tagged **v1.34.1** and attach `module.zip` and `module.json`. The manifest expects:

`https://github.com/pacts-and-polyhedrals/wayreth-black-master/releases/download/v1.34.1/module.zip`

The tag must match the version in `module.json` exactly, `v` prefix included, and the assets must be named `module.zip` and `module.json` exactly. Attaching a newer `module.json` to an older release is the same mistake wearing a different hat: Foundry reads the new version out of the manifest, asks for that tag, and GitHub 404s.

A mismatch is not reported as a missing file. The Forge shows:

```
GET https://forge-vtt.com/api/task/status/... 500 (Internal Server Error)
```

Check before and after publishing:

```bash
npm run check:release
```

It prints the tag and asset names the manifest requires, then probes the live URLs and says which half is wrong. `npm run validate:all` runs its offline half.

## Source and campaign adaptation

The campaign uses *Towers of High Sorcery* (Sovereign Press, 2004) and *The Last Tower: The Legacy of Raistlin* (TSR, 1996) as lore references for institutions, histories, Tower structures, guardian forests and magical locations. The campaign's 350–351 AC chronology deliberately diverges from published history: Caramon's death at Silvanesti, Wayreth choosing Raistlin early, and Raistlin reaching the Palanthas prophecy during the War of the Lance are the spine of this alternate timeline.

**Not included.** The module does not reproduce either source book. No text, table, stat block, illustration or map from *Towers of High Sorcery* or *The Last Tower* is copied into it. Every journal page, spell, item and stat block here is written for this campaign, and the two books are referenced only for names, institutions and geography. The 3.5e prestige classes, the published Test scenarios and the original adventure text are not converted and are not present.

The module is private campaign material and is not for distribution.
